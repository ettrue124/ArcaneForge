### C. Critical Discovery: GroupReq vs. OrderReq

One of the most important findings was that these requests have different purposes.

**GroupReq — Protocol ID 11**

Supports:

- Scry.
- Surveil.
- London mulligan.
- Other supported card-grouping interactions.

For scry, Arena needs a request that allows the player to divide cards between the top and bottom of their library.

**OrderReq — Protocol ID 17**

Supports:

- Ordinary ordering.
- Ordering cards on top.
- Ordering cards on bottom.

OrderReq rearranges cards within a specified destination.

It does not inherently support dividing cards between two destinations.

**Previous bug:** Scry was incorrectly using an ordering menu, making it impossible to drag cards to the bottom of the library.

**Correct approach:** Use GroupReq with the appropriate scry context.

Preserve this distinction.

## 7. Important Message Responsibilities

### GameStateMessage

Responsible for representing and updating:

- Cards and zones.
- Life totals.
- Counters.
- Turn and phase information.
- Stack contents.
- Other gameplay state.

Game-state annotations are also important for animations.

Tapping, life changes, reveals, and energy changes generally rely on game-state updates and annotations rather than standalone menu requests.

### ActionsAvailableReq

Communicates available player actions, including:

- Casting spells.
- Playing lands.
- Activating abilities.
- Passing priority.

Actions must correspond to valid rules-engine states.

### SelectNReq

General-purpose request for choosing a specified number of cards or options.

### SearchReq

Used for interactions such as searching a library.

Requires integration with the rules engine so the player's selection actually determines the searched card.

### SearchFromGroupsReq

Used for search interactions involving grouped choices.

### CastingTimeOptionsReq

Used for choices made during spell casting.

Exact choices depend on the request's payload.

### PayCostsReq

Handles payment decisions for spells and abilities.

The selected payment must be validated and applied by the game engine.

### SelectReplacementReq

Lets the player select among applicable replacement effects.

The existing rules bridge previously selected the first option automatically. A correction was reportedly implemented.

### DistributionReq

Handles distributing quantities among recipients, such as damage or other amounts.

### NumericInputReq

Handles player-entered numerical choices.

### SelectCountersReq

Handles counter selections.

### StringInputReq

Handles text input.

### GroupReq

Used for grouped card choices, including scry, surveil, and London mulligan.

### OrderReq

Handles rearranging the order of cards within a destination.

### IntermissionReq

Supports interactions between games in a match.

### SubmitDeckReq / SubmitDeckConfirmation

Support deck submission and potentially between-game deck changes.

### TimeoutMessage / TimerStateMessage

Provide timeout and timer-related functionality.

These are relevant to the user's requirement for timed matches.

## 8. Implementation Requirements

For every supported request or interaction:

**Step 1 — Identify the correct protocol message.**

Determine the appropriate request type and any required enum values.

**Step 2 — Inspect the real Arena client handler.**

Determine which fields are required for the intended interface.

Do not assume a message's behavior based exclusively on its name.

**Step 3 — Generate the correct request data.**

The request should correspond to the actual game state and legal choices.

**Step 4 — Handle the client's response.**

Properly interpret the player's selection or decision.

**Step 5 — Apply the decision to the rules engine.**

The interaction must affect gameplay correctly.

**Step 6 — Send appropriate state updates.**

Update cards, zones, counters, life totals, stack contents, or other state as necessary.

**Step 7 — Test the complete interaction.**

Verify that the correct menu appears, the player can make a choice, and gameplay continues correctly.

A menu appearing without functioning response handling is not considered complete.
